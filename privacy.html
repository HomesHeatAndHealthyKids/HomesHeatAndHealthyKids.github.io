Homes, Heat and Healthy Kids Study

Privacy Notice

Purpose

The Homes, Heat and Healthy Kids (HHHK) study is a population-based research study led by the University of Edinburgh. The purpose of the study is to better understand how housing conditions and the ability to adequately heat homes are associated with respiratory infections and related health outcomes in young children in Scotland.

Old, damp and under-heated homes are known to be associated with poorer child health, but there is limited evidence quantifying these effects at population level, identifying which children are most affected, or assessing how housing and energy efficiency characteristics modify health risks. The HHHK study aims to address these gaps to support evidence-based policy and service planning in housing, energy and child health.

The study uses existing administrative data that have already been collected by public sector organisations for purposes such as healthcare delivery, civil registration, housing and public services. No new data are collected directly from children or families.

Data Controllers and Responsibilities

The University of Edinburgh is the data controller for the research study, meaning it is responsible for determining the purposes and methods of the research analysis.

Each organisation that provides data to the study (for example NHS Boards, Public Health Scotland, National Records of Scotland, local authorities, and other public sector bodies) remains the data controller for its own dataset. These organisations determine why and how the data are originally collected.

Data are made available to approved researchers only within a secure Trusted Research Environment (Safe Haven). The Safe Haven acts as a data processor and does not determine the purposes of the research.

What Data Are Used

The HHHK study uses a combination of health, demographic, housing and environmental data, which may include:

  • health records relating to hospital admissions, outpatient attendances and GP consultations
  • birth and demographic information
  • housing characteristics and energy efficiency information linked to residential properties
  • area-level information such as deprivation, environmental conditions and energy costs
  • grouped financial and economic indicators derived from commercial data sources

Where individual-level data are used, they are pseudonymised before researchers access them, this means researchers do not have access to names, addresses or other direct identifiers. Some datasets are provided only in grouped or area-level form and do not contain personal data.

How the Data Are Used and Protected

Data linkage is carried out by trusted organisations using established governance processes. Researchers access the data only within a secure Safe Haven environment and only for the approved purposes of the HHHK study.

All researchers are appropriately trained and authorised. Data are not removed from the Safe Haven, and all outputs are checked to ensure that individuals, households or small groups cannot be identified. Results from the study will be reported only in summary or statistical form.

Legal Basis for Processing Data

The HHHK study processes data in accordance with the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018.

Legal basis for processing personal data:

Article 6(1)(e) processing is necessary for the performance of a task carried out in the public interest.

Legal basis for processing special category (health) data:

Article 9(2)(j) processing is necessary for scientific research purposes, subject to appropriate safeguards.

Data Protection Act 2018 condition:

Schedule 1, Part 1, Paragraph 4 research purposes, with safeguards such as data minimisation, pseudonymisation, and restrictions on re-identification.

Retention Period

Data accessed within the Safe Haven are retained only for the duration approved for the HHHK study. At the end of the approved retention period, access to the data will be terminated in accordance with Safe Haven policies and data sharing agreements. Research outputs will not contain personal data and may be retained indefinitely for academic and policy purposes.

Data Sharing

Personal data used in the HHHK study are not shared outside the Safe Haven. Data are not used for marketing, profiling, or commercial purposes. Anonymised or aggregated results may be shared through academic publications, policy reports, and public engagement materials.

Your rights

Data protection law gives you a number of rights, including the right:

  • to be informed about how your personal data are used
  • to object to certain types of processing
  • to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO)

Because this study uses pseudonymised data for research in the public interest, and researchers cannot identify individuals, some rights (such as access to individual records) may not apply in the usual way.
